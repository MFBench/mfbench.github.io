Why MFBench

Actionable language is not the same as grounded judgment.

Cities manage fire risk across dense buildings, interdependent infrastructure, transport systems and expanding wildland–urban interfaces. Multimodal large language models can turn visual inputs into fluent recommendations, but fluency alone does not establish that a response is tied to the people, hazards and spatial relationships visible in the scene.

MFBench separates these properties. It links fire-domain knowledge, structured image- and video-based questions and rubric-scored open-ended fire-scene analysis within one diagnostic benchmark.

Benchmark design

Three stages, one diagnostic question

Does structured competence remain grounded when models must reason and communicate under incomplete evidence?

The Stage 3 test

When evidence is incomplete, will a model acknowledge what it cannot know, or generate a professional-sounding and actionable judgment without adequate visual grounding?

One image, by design

One image is retained from each independent fire incident. This broadens incident coverage, avoids repeatedly weighting image-rich fires and creates the same auditable evidence boundary for every model.

MFBench does not assume that one image is sufficient for complete incident assessment. It tests whether models separate observation from inference and report unresolved information.

System overview

From benchmark diagnosis to governance interpretation

MFBench follows the path from structured knowledge to open-ended analysis, then translates task-specific score profiles into bounded governance hypotheses rather than deployment claims.

Governance translation

A consequence-first framework for human oversight

Benchmark scores inform the framework; they do not authorize operational deployment.

Tier A

Low-consequence support

Information assistance and drafting within clear task boundaries, with human review before release.

Tier B

Expert-verified analysis

Spread, risk and action-related outputs remain advisory and require qualified verification.

Tier C

Non-delegable command

Evacuation orders, entry and suppression strategy, and live resource dispatch remain human decisions.
